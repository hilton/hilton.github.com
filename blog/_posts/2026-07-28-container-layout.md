# Shipping container library layout
**bookcasemaxxing by numbers**

![](billy.jpg)

[Semyon Borisov](https://unsplash.com/photos/KgVwQ5NpoLw){:photocredit}

If you liked the idea of a personal [shipping container library](https://hilton.org.uk/blog/container-library)
in a 20-foot intermodal container that you rent at a self-storage facility, perhaps you want to know how many books you’d have room for.

## Size constraints

Starting with a shipping container gives you a fixed amount of space to work with:

* 5.867 m long
* 2.330 m wide
* 2.350 m high

For our purposes, we’ll use the [IKEA BILLY](https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/cat/billy-series-28102/)
as our reference bookcase, with dimensions:

* 0.80 m wide
* 0.28 m deep
* 2.02 m high

Note that this means that in a bare container, you’ll have a spare 33 cm above each bookcase.
Tragically, this means that a 35 cm
[BILLY height extension unit](https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/p/billy-height-extension-unit-white-40263853/) won’t quite fit.
A proper floor-to-ceiling bookcase will require a custom solution.
Still, at least this means that you have room to install a nicer floor in your container.

You also need room to stand in front of each bookcase, far enough back so you can read the spines on the top shelf,
and with space to kneel on the floor to get to the bottom shelf.
Let’s assume that we need 80 cm – one bookcase width – in front of each bookcase.

## Minimum viable bookcase

The easiest layout lines the walls with bookcases.
This gives you 16 bookcases in the following plan, with the container doors opening on the left (not shown).

![Diagram showing 16 bookcases lining the container walls](container/edges.webp)

If you look closely, you’ll see that bookcases 7 and 8 don’t meet exactly at the corners.
The container’s length means that bookcase 8 overlaps 1.3 cm of the right-hand edge of bookcase 7.
Fortunately, this doesn’t matter, because BILLY bookcases have 2 cm-thick side panels, so the overlap doesn’t obstruct any books.
We’ll ignore the [BILLY corner unit](https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/p/billy-bookcase-combination-crnr-solution-white-s99395936/),
because that stores fewer books.

The light grey areas show the 80 cm reserved space in front of each bookcase.
Unsatisfyingly, this leaves a 17 cm gap between bookcases 8 and 9, and roughly 1 m² unused ‘white space’.
We can add more bookcases.

## More bookcases

We can try adding bookcases, because we’ve got some unused floorspace,
and because bookcases that face each other can share _access space_.
For example, five more bookcases along the middle:

![Diagram showing five more bookcases in the middle](container/middle.webp)

This might feel cramped, in practice, because the bookcases’ 28 cm depth exceeds the 17 cm gap.
Maybe this doesn’t matter if you decide not admit guests to your private library, 
although the space at the front would allow a one-way system, even when you close the doors.

21 bookcases looks good, but the boring layout less so.
This doesn’t leave much space for an armchair either.
Let’s try a more interesting layout.

## Cosy alcoves

Arranging bookcases into U-shapes creates alcoves that increase the book density,
so you get 11 bookcases along one wall.

![Diagram showing alcoves along one wall](container/alcoves-top.webp)

The container’s width doesn’t allow for alcoves the same size on both sides,
but we can use the 40 cm half-width BILLY bookcases to make a shallow alcove.
We can also swap one pair of half-width bookcases to make a zig-zag central corridor.

![Diagram showing a more interesting layout for 21 bookcases](container/alcoves.webp)

The larger 1.6 m × 1.6 m square area at the end furthest from the doors (right) gives you enough space for a small rug and an armchair,
for additional [hygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hygge).
Besides, you probably need somewhere to sit, while you work out how many books you can store like this.
