In this series, I’ve established that a [shipping container library](container-library)
with an [interesting layout](container-layout) can
[hold more than 5000 books](container-capacity).
A five kilobook personal library may seem unnecessary,
since you probably can’t read that many books in your lifetime.
However, even if [book people](https://www.instagram.com/yayalexisgay/reel/C2NZhzJrXQi/)
do prefer reading, libraries become fundamentally social places.
The books want you to share them.

## Book Aid International

Book Aid International knows why you need 5000+ books in a shipping container: for
[Community Container Libraries](https://bookaid.org/what-we-do/supporting-libraries/community-container-libraries/)
that ‘bring more books to the communities worldwide that will benefit from them the most’.
(Reassuringly, they also arrive at the same 5000+ capacity, despite adding windows.)

They’ve figured out how to source, install, and stock a shipping container library, such as the one pictured (photo, above) in Dunga, Zanzibar (population 76 000).
This kind of custom [cargotecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipping_container_architecture)
probably poses challenges that require expertise and persistence to solve, as well as a flexible budget.
This sounds like an opportunity for scaling and standardisation.

## Tiny homes

So far, the [tiny-house movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny-house_movement)
has probably got us the closest to productising container-sized buildings.
The niche idea of living in a really small home, for a small-footprint lifestyle,
offers some combination of simpler, cheaper, location-flexible or more sustainable living.

Tiny houses can also offer designer luxury: browsing
[26 Modern Tiny House Design Inspirations For Your Tiny Home](https://www.tinyeasy.com/modern-tiny-house-design)
gives you more of a chic holiday home vibe than one about economically downsizing.
You also get the Scandinavian design angle, which sells well,
and makes sense given the market for well-off Scandinavian’s
[summer houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_house#In_the_Nordic_countries).

But when you browse [beautiful tiny house photos](https://www.studioelevene.com/tiny-house),
you realise the terrible cost of the small-footprint lifestyle.
**Tiny houses don’t have room for bookcases.**

## Tiny personal libraries

Start-up company idea: solve the problem of moving in to a tiny house
and realising that you can’t live without a substantial quantity of books,
for rich book people who like expensive design.
While committing to a tiny-house lifestyle, they also think that _books don’t count_.

The successful _tiny library_ start-up won’t settle for squeezing an unsatisfyingly small bookcase into a conventional tiny house design.
Instead, they’ll sell you a container-sized add-on unit, kitted out as a luxury personal library,
to connect to your tiny house.
It will have enough room for all of your books, plus some kind of secondary collection.
Whisky, maybe.

## Tiny village libraries

A successful ‘hardback’ product for the luxury market might generate significant revenue,
design and construction expertise, and economies of scale.
This could fund a more affordable ‘paperback’ product – tiny libraries for more price-sensitive markets:

* **young professionals** with disposable income, but not enough to buy a home big enough for their growing book collection
* **holiday parks**, because now matter how good their facilities, when you run out of books to read in the middle of your summer holiday, they never seem to have a decent library
* **tiny house collectives**, such as Rotterdam’s experimental [De Kleine Burg](https://www.rotterdamarchitectuurprijs.nl/archief/de-kleine-burg.html) (in Dutch), who want to share a _tiny village library_.

And these, of course, would only represent the next milestone on the ultimate business plan.

## Community libraries

Mass-market tiny libraries would commoditise their construction.
And best of all, they could fund widespread and cheaper community libraries for Book Aid International, and other charities.
