As autumn approaches, I start thinking about comfy jumpers, the big coat, and hot comfort food for lunch.
Macaroni cheese’s extreme blandness delivers hot comfort food with low cognitive load,
ideal for a lunch break on a busy working day.

As a bonus, cooking a single portion from time to time lets you practice making a cheese sauce,
which you can use in other classic bland English recipes,
such as [cauliflower cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauliflower_cheese).

## Ingredients – serves one

A single portion makes the dish faster and easier to prepare: less cheese to grate, and a lower risk of lumpy sauce.

* 80 g [straight macaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaroni)
* 10 g plain flour
* 10 g butter
* 100 ml milk
* 50 g mature cheddar, grated

## Method

With practice, you can work faster, and finish the sauce before the pasta has finished cooking.

1. Boil the macaroni according to the direction on the packet
2. Put a small non-stick saucepan on a low heat.
3. Add the butter and flour.
4. Make a [roux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roux) by mixing with a small (ideally silicone) spatula until no loose flour remains, and the mixture forms a single smooth lump.
5. Add a splash of milk, and stir into the mixture until it returns to a single smooth consistency.
6. Make a [béchamel sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9chamel_sauce), by repeating the previous step until you have used all of the milk, taking care to avoid lumps.
7. Make a cheese sauce, by stirring in the grated cheese.
8. When the macaroni has finished cooking, drain and stir into the cheese sauce.

## Garnish (optional)

To add a contrast to the dish’s blandness, you can garnish the dish with a generous sprinkling of one of the following.

* Course-ground black pepper.
* Crispy fried onion (as used in South Asian rice dishes).
* Chopped fresh chives.

## Tips

* After draining the macaroni, let it stand in the colander/sieve for 1 minute, to release steam and dry out, too avoid making the sauce runnier.
* Experiment with more sauce for the same amount of pasta.
* Experiment with more or less milk, to make the sauce runnier or thicker.

## Variations

Take care when experimenting with extra ingredients, because strong flavours will spoil the dish’s signature blandness.
Try adding _one_ of the following.

* _Mild_ instead of _mature_ cheddar, to make it more bland.
* ¼ tsp mustard powder or English mustard, to make it less bland.
* ½ tsp turmeric, to make it more yellow, with a subtle extra flavour.
* Smoked salmon or bacon bits, to make it more tasty.
* Rendered bacon fat instead of butter, to make it less healthy.
* Seafood or shellfish, e.g. prawns, crab or lobster, to make it more fancy.

Unacceptable variations use cheese other than cheddar, which I consider _incorrect_,
not least because a different cheese flavour fails to deliver the same nostalgic feeling.
As a small child and fussy eater, I loved English-style macaroni cheese, precisely for its blandness.
